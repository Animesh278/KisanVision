**Subject**: Potato Foliar and Tuber Disease Identification and Integrated Management

## Section 1: Potato Early Blight (Alternaria solani)
### Etiology and Diagnostics
Early blight affects potato foliage and tubers throughout temperate growing regions.
- **Visual Symptoms**: Symptoms initiate as small, brownish-black spots on older, senescing lower leaves. As spots enlarge (6 to 12 mm), they develop characteristic dark concentric rings resembling a miniature target. Lesions are often bounded by major leaf veins, imparting an angular shape. Chlorotic yellow halos surround active lesions. On tubers, lesions appear brown to black, slightly sunken, circular with raised margins, and underlying flesh is dry, brown, and corky.
- **Favorable Environmental Conditions**: Warm weather (20°C to 27°C) with alternating wet and dry cycles. Stressed potato crops (nitrogen deficiency, nematode damage, or drought stress) exhibit increased susceptibility.
- **Distinction from Late Blight**: Early blight lesions are dry and leathery with concentric rings, whereas Late Blight lesions are water-soaked, lack rings, and produce white downy mold underneath during damp periods.

### Integrated Management and Next Steps
1. **Nutritional Optimization**: Maintain adequate nitrogen and potassium levels; vigorous, well-nourished canopies resist early blight significantly better than nutrient-stressed crops.
2. **Foliar Fungicide Regimen**: Initiate protective fungicide applications (chlorothalonil, mancozeb, or azoxystrobin/difenoconazole) beginning around row closure or flowering when lower leaves remain moist. Rotate FRAC chemical classes.
3. **Harvest and Storage Protocol**: Allow potato vines to completely die and skins to set for 14-21 days prior to harvest. Avoid mechanical tuber bruising during digging.

## Section 2: Potato Late Blight (Phytophthora infestans)
### Etiology and Diagnostics
Late blight is the most notorious potato disease, responsible for catastrophic yield loss in both fields and storage bins.
- **Visual Symptoms**: Appears first as water-soaked, dark-green to purplish-black spots, typically beginning at leaf tips or margins where dew persists. Under humid conditions (>90% RH), a delicate white fungal-like sporulation is visible on the abaxial leaf surface along the margins of the dead tissue. Petioles and stems develop water-soaked dark brown girdling lesions, making plants prone to lodging. Infected tubers have brown, reddish-brown, or purplish surface skin discoloration with dry, granular, brick-red rot extending into the flesh.
- **Favorable Environmental Conditions**: Cool, wet, foggy, or humid conditions (temperatures between 12°C and 20°C).

### Integrated Management and Next Steps
1. **Destruction of Inoculum Sources**: Destroy cull piles, volunteer potato plants, and infected seed tubers prior to the season. Never plant tubers showing any symptoms of late blight.
2. **Preventative Chemical Protection**: Apply protectant fungicides (mancozeb, chlorothalonil, or copper) before infection occurs when weather forecasting models indicate high disease severity values (DSVs).
3. **Emergency Interventions**: In confirmed field strikes, apply translaminar or systemic oomyceticides (e.g., dimethomorph, fluopicolide, cyazofamid). Kill vines completely (mechanically or with registered desiccants) at least 2 weeks before harvest to prevent spore washdown onto tubers.

## Section 3: Healthy Potato Agronomic Standards
### Optimal Crop Development Indicators
A healthy potato canopy exhibits:
- Uniform dark green foliage without chlorosis, marginal scorch, or mosaic patterns.
- Vigorous upright stems with dense canopy closure.
- Balanced tuber initiation coinciding with flowering.
- Proper soil moisture management maintaining 65-75% available water capacity.
- Routine pest scouting for Colorado potato beetle and potato leafhopper.
