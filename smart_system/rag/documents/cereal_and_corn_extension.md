## Section 1: Corn Northern Leaf Blight (Exserohilum turcicum)
### Etiology and Diagnostics
Northern corn leaf blight (NCLB) is caused by the fungus *Exserohilum turcicum* (syn. *Setosphaeria turcica*).
- **Visual Symptoms**: Characteristic long, elliptical, grayish-green to tan lesions measuring 2.5 to 15 cm in length and 1 to 2.5 cm in width ("cigar-shaped" or "spindle-shaped"). Lesions begin on lower leaves and move upward. In moist weather, dark greenish-black fungal sporulation develops on lesion centers. Entire leaves can wither and die prematurely if lesions coalesce.
- **Favorable Environmental Conditions**: Moderate temperatures (18°C to 27°C / 65°F to 80°F) accompanied by 6 to 12 hours of leaf wetness from rain or heavy dew.
- **Distinction from Gray Leaf Spot**: Gray leaf spot produces rectangular lesions with blunt, squared ends strictly delimited by leaf veins; NCLB lesions are significantly longer and elliptical with tapered ends.

### Integrated Management and Next Steps
1. **Hybrid Selection**: Plant corn hybrids with quantitative polygenic resistance or qualitative Ht-gene resistance.
2. **Residue Management & Rotation**: Practice minimum 1-year crop rotation out of corn; incorporate or shred corn stubble in conventional tillage systems.
3. **Fungicide Application**: In high-risk fields with susceptible hybrids, apply registered dual-action fungicides (strobilurin + triazole / SDHI mixtures) between VT (tassel) and R1 (silking) stages if lesions appear on the third leaf below the ear leaf or higher.

## Section 2: Corn Common Rust (Puccinia sorghi)
### Etiology and Diagnostics
Common rust is caused by the biotrophic fungus *Puccinia sorghi*.
- **Visual Symptoms**: Small, powdery, circular to elongate reddish-brown to cinnamon-brown pustules (uredinia) scattered across both upper (adaxial) and lower (abaxial) leaf surfaces. As leaves mature, pustules rupture the epidermal layer, shedding rust-colored powdery spores. Later in the season, pustules turn black as teliospores develop.
- **Distinction from Southern Rust**: Southern rust (*Puccinia polysora*) forms smaller, tightly clustered, golden-orange pustules predominantly on the upper leaf surface only.

### Integrated Management and Next Steps
1. **Cultivar Resistance**: Most modern temperate commercial hybrids carry strong genetic resistance (Rp genes).
2. **Chemical Control**: Rarely required unless susceptible inbred lines or sweet corn varieties are grown and pustules develop prior to tassel under cool (16°C to 25°C), humid weather.

## Section 3: Rice Bacterial Leaf Blight (Xanthomonas oryzae pv. oryzae)
### Etiology and Diagnostics
Bacterial leaf blight (BLB) is a destructive bacterial vascular disease of wetland rice.
- **Visual Symptoms**: Symptoms appear on leaf blades as water-soaked translucent stripes along leaf margins. These stripes rapidly enlarge, turn yellow to grayish-white with wavy or undulating margins, and progress down the length of the blade. Droplets of bacterial exudate (milky or amber ooze) appear on young lesions in early morning dew. The systemic "kresek" phase causes seedling wilting and death within 2-3 weeks of transplanting.
- **Favorable Conditions**: Warm temperatures (25°C to 34°C), high humidity, typhoons/floods, and excessive application of nitrogen fertilizer.

### Integrated Management and Next Steps
1. **Balanced Nutrition**: Strictly avoid excessive top-dressing with nitrogen; ensure balanced potash (K) fertilization which strengthens cell wall silicon/lignin defenses.
2. **Water Management**: Drain infected paddies temporarily to reduce field-to-field splash spread. Avoid deep standing water.
3. **Resistant Cultivars**: Cultivate rice varieties bearing verified Xa resistance genes (e.g., Xa4, Xa21).
4. **Biocontrol**: Foliar sprays of antagonistic rhizobacteria (*Pseudomonas fluorescens* or *Bacillus velezensis*) at tillering.

## Section 4: Rice Leaf Blast (Magnaporthe oryzae / Pyricularia oryzae)
### Etiology and Diagnostics
Blast affects all above-ground tissues of rice, including leaves, nodes, panicle neck, and grains.
- **Visual Symptoms**: On leaves, initial lesions are small, water-soaked bluish-green specks that broaden into spindle-shaped or diamond-shaped spots (elliptic with pointed ends). Fully developed lesions have gray or white necrotic centers surrounded by narrow dark brown to reddish-brown margins with chlorotic yellow haloes. Neck blast causes blackening of the panicle base and unfilled whiteheads.
- **Favorable Conditions**: Overcast skies, cool night temperatures (17°C to 23°C) with warm days, relative humidity exceeding 90%, and drought-induced aerobic soil stress.

### Integrated Management and Next Steps
1. **Water & Soil Management**: Maintain continuous shallow flooding; avoid letting paddies dry out and crack, as drought stress exacerbates blast susceptibility.
2. **Fungicide Protection**: At early spotting or pre-heading (booting) stage, apply registered protective/curative blast fungicides such as tricyclazole, isoprothiolane, or azoxystrobin.
